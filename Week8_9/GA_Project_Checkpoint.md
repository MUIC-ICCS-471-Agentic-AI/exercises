# ICCS471 — Term Project Checkpoint

**Team assignment · 20 points**  
**In-class review: Friday, 27 November.**  
**Assessment: live demonstration and conversation; no submission required.**

Show what your team has built, how you checked it, and what each member has contributed. This is a progress review: unfinished features and known defects are expected.

## 1. Prepare for the conversation

**>> TODO — Bring your application and repository ready for a 15-minute team conversation.**

Prepare to show and explain:

- **One working part:** Demonstrate a meaningful action or input leading to an observable result. Explain its purpose and current limitations. Both core features do not need to be complete yet; identify simulated behaviour.
- **One check:** Show the input/action, expected outcome, actual result and how to repeat it. An automated test or concrete manual check is sufficient.
- **One review decision:** Show code, a test or an AI suggestion that a member inspected. Explain what they accepted, changed or rejected and why.
- **Each person's contribution:** Every member briefly explains their responsibility, technical work completed so far, and next task. Open one relevant code, test or commit reference for each person.

> **Example:** Show an order being submitted and appearing in the staff queue. Then show what happens when quantity 0 is entered. Explain one implementation or testing decision your team made. Updating order status may still be unfinished.

Implementation, debugging, testing and integration all count as technical work. Identify each person's part in shared work. The lecturer will consider GitHub history and your explanations together; **commit counts alone do not determine contribution.**

If blocked, show your implementation attempt and a reproducible failure, explain what you tried, and identify the next investigation. Credit remains available for the work and reasoning you can demonstrate.

## 2. During the review

| Approximate time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 4 minutes | Demonstrate the working part and explain its limitations. |
| 2 minutes | Show the check and explain the review decision. |
| 5–6 minutes | Each member explains their contribution and answers a question. |
| 3 minutes | Discuss feedback and agree on next actions. |

All members should attend. If someone cannot attend, contact the lecturer to arrange how they will provide their explanation. Use the application and repository directly; no slide deck is needed.

## 3. Assessment during class

Your team receives one mark based on what you demonstrate and explain during the conversation. Have the current application and relevant code, tests and GitHub history ready to open. You can refer to your existing project documents; no Classroom submission is required.

Explain the selected check and review decision aloud and show the supporting evidence directly. Each member explains their own contribution. The lecturer will record the rubric scores and give feedback during the session.

## 4. Rubric — 20 points

The team shares one checkpoint mark, assessed during the session. Individual technical contribution and understanding are also assessed separately through the project's individual defence and development evidence.

Each row is worth **5 points**. Full marks describe good progress at this stage, not a finished product. Each row is assessed independently: an unfinished feature does not automatically lose marks for checking, contribution explanations or sensible planning.

| Criterion | 5 — Strong | 4 — Competent | 2–3 — Partial | 1 — Limited | 0 — Missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Working progress | A meaningful part of the intended workflow runs from action/input to a useful result. Its purpose and gaps are clear. | Useful working behaviour with a minor defect or demonstration gap. | Relevant implementation exists, but the result is incomplete, fragile or only partly demonstrated. | Minimal implementation; mainly static screens or plans. | No assessable implementation attempt. |

| Member contributions and understanding | Every member identifies meaningful technical work, shows a relevant reference and explains their part accurately. Shared work is attributed clearly. | Contributions and understanding are evident across the team, with a minor reference or explanation gap. | Some contributions are clear, but one or more members' work or understanding remains insufficiently supported. | Most members cannot identify or explain concrete technical contributions. | No assessable contribution evidence or explanations. |
| Status and next steps | Current limitations are clear, and feasible next actions address the main remaining work with named responsibilities. | Mostly clear status and practical next steps, with a minor gap. | Some useful planning, but important gaps, priorities or responsibilities are unclear. | Vague status or next steps disconnected from the current work. | No assessable status or next steps. |
